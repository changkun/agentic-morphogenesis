\section{Formalization}

The aim of this section is not to claim a complete theory, but to convert the paper's central constructs into measurable objects. A more scientific account of agentic morphogenesis requires definitions that support logging, comparison across runs, and eventual hypothesis testing.

\subsection{System State, Outcomes, and Bottleneck Migration}

We model an agentic system at round $t$ by a state
\begin{equation}
S_t = (A_t, W_t, M_t, H_t),
\end{equation}
where $A_t$ is the architecture, $W_t$ is the workload or task distribution, $M_t$ is the persistent artifact state (code, plans, documentation, logs), and $H_t$ is the available human-oversight budget. The architecture is a directed role graph
\begin{equation}
A_t = (R_t, E_t, B_t),
\end{equation}
with roles $R_t$, communication edges $E_t$, and role-specific context or bandwidth budgets $B_t$.

Each round yields a vector of observables
\begin{equation}
y_t = (q_t, v_t, c_t, h_t) \in \mathbb{R}^4,
\end{equation}
where $q_t$ denotes quality or correctness, $v_t$ delivery velocity, $c_t$ coordination cost, and $h_t$ human attention consumed. The specific metrics may differ by deployment, but the important point is that performance is \emph{multi-objective}. No single scalar captures system health without contestable weighting assumptions.

Let $\mathcal{D}$ index monitored dimensions and let $s_d(y_t)$ be the normalized slack of dimension $d$ relative to an unacceptable region or target threshold. The active bottleneck at round $t$ is
\begin{equation}
b_t = \arg\min_{d \in \mathcal{D}} s_d(y_t).
\end{equation}
Suppose an intervention $\pi_t$ changes the architecture from $A_t$ to $A_{t+1}$. We say that \emph{bottleneck migration} occurs when the targeted dimension improves by at least $\epsilon > 0$ but the active bottleneck changes:
\begin{equation}
s_{b_t}(y_{t+1}) - s_{b_t}(y_t) \geq \epsilon
\quad \text{and} \quad
b_{t+1} \neq b_t.
\end{equation}
This definition makes the concept operational: migration is not merely any post hoc story about change, but a measurable shift in the most constrained dimension after a structural intervention.

\subsection{Role Fission as a Capacity-Interference Tradeoff}

The role-fission hypothesis can be stated as a comparison between overloaded local cognition and added coordination cost. For a role $r \in R_t$ with responsibility set $U_r$, define its effective cognitive load as
\begin{equation}
L_r(t) = \sum_{u \in U_r} \ell(u,t) + \sum_{\substack{u,v \in U_r \\ u \neq v}} \iota(u,v,t),
\end{equation}
where $\ell(u,t)$ is the context demand of responsibility $u$ and $\iota(u,v,t)$ captures interference between responsibilities that compete for the same context window or working memory. Let $B_r(t)$ denote the role's usable context budget and let $\Gamma(r_1,r_2,t)$ denote the communication overhead introduced by splitting $r$ into two roles $r_1$ and $r_2$.

Fission is predicted to be beneficial when the overload penalty exceeds the induced coordination cost:
\begin{equation}
L_r(t) - B_r(t) > \lambda \, \Gamma(r_1,r_2,t),
\end{equation}
for some deployment-dependent conversion factor $\lambda > 0$. This formalization is intentionally simple, but it makes several qualitative predictions clear.

First, increasing context budget should delay the onset of fission. Second, improving summarization or memory artifacts should reduce the interference term $\iota(\cdot,\cdot,t)$ and can therefore substitute for some role splits. Third, as communication bandwidth degrades, the right-hand side grows and the net benefit of additional specialization should eventually reverse. These are experimentally testable claims, not merely interpretive analogies.

\subsection{Trust Calibration as Preference Learning with Escalation}

The autonomy boundary between an agentic system and its human overseers can be modeled as a context-dependent abstention problem. Let $\mathcal{X}$ denote a decision-context space derived from system state:
\begin{equation}
x_t = \phi(S_t, a_t),
\end{equation}
where $a_t$ is a candidate action and $\phi$ extracts relevant features such as task novelty, architectural configuration, recent failure history, and expected blast radius. Define a latent function
\begin{equation}
f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R},
\end{equation}
where $f(x)$ is the expected advantage of autonomous execution over human consultation in context $x$. We place a Gaussian Process prior
\begin{equation}
f \sim \mathcal{GP}(\mu_0, k),
\end{equation}
with conservative prior mean $\mu_0$ and kernel $k$.

As in standard preference learning \cite{chu2005,houlsby2011}, we assume observations arrive as pairwise comparisons rather than calibrated scalar rewards:
\begin{equation}
z_{ij} =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if outcome at } x_i \text{ is preferred to outcome at } x_j, \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Using a probit likelihood,
\begin{equation}
P(z_{ij}=1 \mid f) =
\Phi\!\left(\frac{f(x_i)-f(x_j)}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right),
\end{equation}
the posterior mean and uncertainty induce an escalation policy
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\mathrm{auto}}(x)=
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mu_t(x)-\beta \sigma_t(x) > \tau\right],
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ controls risk aversion and $\tau$ is the autonomy threshold. The system proceeds autonomously only when the lower confidence bound exceeds the threshold; otherwise it escalates.

This formulation is close in spirit to reject-option learning \cite{bartlett2008}: the key question is not only which action is better, but when to defer. It also aligns with contextual dueling-bandit formulations \cite{dudik2015}, where policy improvement is driven by pairwise preference feedback under changing context.

\subsection{Architecture Search as Constrained Preferential Bayesian Optimization}

If architecture is allowed to vary, the system faces a design problem over configurations $c \in \mathcal{C}$, where $c$ encodes role count, topology, memory structure, and resource allocation. Let $u(c,w)$ denote latent utility under workload $w$, and let $g_j(c,w) \leq 0$ encode operational constraints such as latency, token cost, test-failure rate, or human-review load. Because utility is multi-dimensional and difficult to scalarize honestly, we observe preferences of the form
\begin{equation}
(c_i,w) \succ (c_j,w)
\end{equation}
instead of direct rewards. The natural optimization problem is therefore a constrained version of preferential Bayesian optimization \cite{gonzalez2017,brochu2010}.

Conceptually, an Architect agent maintains a posterior over both utility and feasibility, then proposes the next architecture by balancing exploitation, exploration, and operational safety. This interpretation clarifies what the Architect is and is not. It is not a magical meta-agent that ``knows'' the right topology; it is a sequential experimental design mechanism operating over an architecture space with noisy, preference-based feedback.

\subsection{Falsifiable Implications}

The formalization above yields four concrete predictions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item For a fixed workload, increasing effective context budget should delay or suppress role fission.
\item Summary-producing artifacts should partially substitute for organizational differentiation by reducing interference within a role.
\item Net performance as a function of role count should be non-monotone: early specialization helps, but coordination cost eventually dominates.
\item A learned uncertainty-aware escalation boundary should outperform static HITL rules at the same human-review budget when task contexts are heterogeneous.
\end{enumerate}

These predictions are the main reason to formalize the framework at all. Without them, ``architectural morphogenesis'' would remain a vivid description; with them, it becomes a family of claims that can be contradicted by data.
