\section{Conclusion}

We have presented an empirical systems study, a formal scaffold, and a research agenda for architectural change in autonomous agentic systems. The strongest claims are modest but useful: in the observed runs, bottlenecks did not disappear, they migrated; independent runs did not wander arbitrarily, they converged on a task family and then diverged in specialization; and role differentiation appears to trade local cognitive relief against global coordination cost.

The paper is intentionally explicit about what remains missing. The role-fission principle is a testable hypothesis, not an established law. The Trust GP is a formal proposal, not yet a validated controller. The later sections on populations, self-reference, and morphogenesis as a stable attractor are conjectural extensions that help organize future experiments but currently outrun the data. If subsequent work reproduces the empirical regularities, operationalizes the proposed measurements, and validates the uncertainty-aware escalation model, then the field will have a stronger basis for treating architecture itself as a learnable variable in autonomous agent systems. If those tests fail, the value of the present paper will still be that it states clearly what should be measured next.
